Land surfaces, water, and the satellites that watch them.

I'm a postdoctoral researcher at the Earth and Remote Sensing Laboratory, Sungkyunkwan University, blending satellite remote sensing, in-situ instruments, and land surface modeling to track water through the surface — from precipitation to soil moisture to evapotranspiration.

About

Background

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Earth and Remote Sensing Laboratory (ERSL), Sungkyunkwan University, working with Prof. Minha Choi. My research integrates satellite remote sensing, in-situ observations, and land surface modeling to study land surface–atmosphere interactions and how water moves through the land — from precipitation, to soil moisture and evapotranspiration, to floods and droughts.

Recent threads of my work include high-resolution soil moisture retrieval from Sentinel-1 SAR, multi-scale validation using cosmic-ray neutron probes, and characterizing precipitation forcing uncertainty in land surface models. I am broadly interested in remote sensing, hydrological extremes, and data assimilation across heterogeneous terrains.

Research interests

What I work on

SAR Soil Moisture Retrieval

High-resolution soil moisture from Sentinel-1, temporal stability analysis, physics-informed AI.

Cosmic-Ray Neutron Probe

Intermediate-scale soil moisture, CRNP calibration, multi-scale validation in heterogeneous terrains.

Land Surface Modeling

Noah-MP and LIS-based simulation, precipitation forcing uncertainty, root-zone soil moisture.

Evapotranspiration

Satellite/drone-based ET estimation, energy balance methods, vegetation–water coupling.

Flood & Drought

Flash drought dynamics, SAR water body detection, hydrological extremes in monsoon Asia.

Data Assimilation

Combining satellite, land surface model, and in-situ data through statistical and ML approaches.
